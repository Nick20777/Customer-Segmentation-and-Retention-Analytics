# Customer Segmentation & Retention Analytics
**Turning Retail Transaction Data into Customer Insights**

An independent data analytics project exploring how customer data can support business understanding and decision-making.

## About the Project:
As a Business Sciences student at Sapienza University of Rome, I wanted to explore how data analytics could complement business knowledge and help answer real business questions.

I independently developed this project using the UCI Online Retail dataset, taking raw transaction data and turning it into customer insights through data preparation, RFM segmentation, retention analysis, and an interactive Excel dashboard.

## Key Results:

| Metric | Result |
|---|---:|
| Cleaned Transactions | 397,884 |
| Unique Customers | 4,338 |
| Unique Invoices | 18,532 |
| Total Revenue | £8,911,407.90 |
| Average Revenue per Customer | £2,054.27 |
| Repeat Customer Share | 65.58% |

A few notable findings:

- Champions: 201 customers generated approximately £3.93 million in revenue.
- Potential Loyalists: 1,103 customers represented an opportunity for further engagement.
- Hibernating: 1,378 customers formed a potential re-engagement group.

## Dashboard Preview

The Excel dashboard brings together key business metrics and visualizations covering customer segments, revenue, purchasing frequency, and cohort retention.

## What I Built

- Prepared and transformed transaction data using Power Query.
- Developed customer-level RFM analysis and six rule-based segments.
- Examined one-time versus repeat purchasing behaviour.
- Conducted cohort retention analysis.
- Built an Excel dashboard to communicate findings and potential business opportunities.

## Key Takeaway

This project strengthened my ability to connect business questions with analytical methods. More importantly, it taught me to look beyond the numbers: defining metrics carefully, questioning assumptions, and interpreting results within their limitations.

The recommendations are exploratory and have not been tested through controlled marketing experiments.
